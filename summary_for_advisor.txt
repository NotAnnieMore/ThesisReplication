1. OBJECTIVE
================================================================================

Replicate and extend the methodology of Papaiz et al. (2024) — 
"An ensemble-imbalance machine learning model for the prediction of ALS 
prognosis" — using the PRO-ACT clinical database to predict short-survival 
ALS patients (death <= 24 months from symptom onset).

Key extensions over the original paper:
  - Optuna (Bayesian) replaces Grid Search for hyperparameter optimization
  - LightGBM added as a 7th classifier (paper used 6)
  - 10 individual resampling techniques instead of BalancedBagging
  - Both MLP and LightGBM analyzed with SHAP for comparison

5. MODEL TRAINING — CROSS-VALIDATION (Step 5)
================================================================================

5.1 Experimental Design
────────────────────────
  - 7 classifiers: KNN, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, Random Forest, 
                   SVM, MLP (Neural Network), LightGBM*
  - 10 imbalance handling: None, ROS, SMOTE, BorderlineSMOTE, ADASYN,
                           RUS, NearMiss, TomekLinks, SMOTETomek, SMOTEENN
  - Total: 70 combinations
  - Optimization: Optuna (TPE sampler, 50 trials per combination)
  - CV: RepeatedStratifiedKFold (5 folds × 3 repeats = 15 evaluations)
  - Optimization metric: ROC-AUC

  * LightGBM is our addition (not in the original paper)

  Paper used: Grid Search with pre-defined parameter grids, 6 classifiers.
  Our approach: Bayesian optimization (Optuna), which explores the 
  hyperparameter space more efficiently with fewer evaluations.

CRITICAL FINDING — MLP collapse:
  The MLP, despite having the highest CV AUC (0.915), demonstrates 
  catastrophic failure on the test set: it detects only 1 out of 35 Short 
  patients (recall = 0.029). It predicts virtually every patient as 
  Non-Short. This is a textbook example of a model optimized for a ranking 
  metric (AUC) that fails at the decision threshold in severely imbalanced 
  problems.

  LightGBM+ROS correctly identifies 30 out of 35 Short patients (recall = 
  0.857) while maintaining good specificity (0.831). This makes it the 
  clinically superior model for ALS prognosis prediction.

7b. BONFERRONI STATISTICAL CORRECTION (Step 7c)
================================================================================

To replicate the paper's statistical validation, we performed Wilcoxon 
signed-rank tests comparing Single-Model vs Ensemble-Imbalance (BalancedBagging)
for each classifier, across the 15 CV folds (5×3). p-values are corrected 
with Bonferroni (×7 comparisons).

Script: 02_scripts/step7c_bonferroni.py (new file, does not modify any prior code)

  *** p ≤ 0.001  |  ** p ≤ 0.01  |  * p ≤ 0.05  |  n.s. = not significant

Key findings:
  - 6 out of 7 classifiers show statistically significant improvement with 
    BalancedBagging (p < 0.01 after Bonferroni correction).
  - NaiveBayes is the only exception (p=1.0, n.s.) — it already handles 
    class imbalance reasonably well without ensemble wrapping (ΔBalAcc=+0.01).
  - SVM has the largest absolute improvement (Δ=+0.293), going from 
    BalAcc=0.50 (chance level, predicts all Non-Short) to 0.79.
  - MLP achieves the best ensemble BalAcc (0.832), confirming the paper's 
    finding that MLP+BalancedBagging is the top-performing model.
  - These results are consistent with Papaiz et al. Table 3.

All 3 metrics (Balanced Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity) were tested. 
Sensitivity shows significant improvement for all 7 classifiers (including 
NaiveBayes). Specificity decreases significantly for 6/7 classifiers, 
reflecting the expected trade-off when addressing class imbalance.

9.2 Results Comparison (Paper's BalancedBagging approach):
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Paper's best: MLP + BalancedBagging
    - AUC = 0.93–0.94 (reported)
    - Balanced Accuracy ≈ 0.84

  Our replication: MLP + BalancedBagging
    - Balanced Accuracy = 0.83
    - Sensitivity = 0.84, Specificity = 0.82

  Difference is minimal and explained by:
    - Smaller dataset (1,502 vs 1,967 → 23.6% fewer patients)
    - Higher imbalance ratio (7.6 vs 6.9)
    - Potentially different PRO-ACT data version

9.3 Our Extension — Why LightGBM+ROS is Superior:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
  While the paper correctly identifies MLP+BalancedBagging as optimal in the 
  ensemble-imbalance scenario, our extended analysis reveals a critical 
  limitation of MLP when used with simpler resampling strategies:

  On the held-out test set:
    - MLP (best CV config): AUC=0.918, but Recall=0.029 (1/35 Short detected)
    - LightGBM+ROS:         AUC=0.923, Recall=0.857 (30/35 Short detected)

  The MLP collapses at the decision boundary with an IR of 7.6, predicting 
  nearly all patients as Non-Short. LightGBM+ROS maintains strong 
  performance across all metrics. This demonstrates that:

  1. AUC alone is insufficient for evaluating models on imbalanced data
  2. Simpler resampling (ROS) with a gradient-boosted model can match or 
     exceed the ensemble approach
  3. LightGBM offers exact SHAP computation (TreeExplainer), providing 
     more reliable interpretability than the approximate KernelExplainer 
     required for MLP

9.4 Clinical Relevance:
────────────────────────
  In ALS prognosis, failing to identify a Short-survival patient (False 
  Negative) has serious clinical consequences: missed opportunities for 
  early palliative care, advance directives, and clinical trial enrollment.

  LightGBM+ROS: 5 missed Short patients out of 35 (FN rate = 14.3%)
  MLP:          34 missed Short patients out of 35 (FN rate = 97.1%)

  The LightGBM model is clearly preferable for clinical deployment.


================================================================================
10. CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

1. REPLICATION SUCCESSFUL: We successfully replicated the Papaiz et al. 
   pipeline with comparable results. Our MLP+BalancedBagging achieves 
   BalAcc=0.83, close to the paper's reported performance.

2. NOVEL CONTRIBUTION — LightGBM: Adding LightGBM as a 7th classifier 
   proved highly valuable. With simple Random Oversampling (ROS), it 
   achieves the best test-set AUC (0.923) and the best clinical trade-off 
   (Recall=0.86, Specificity=0.83).

3. OPTUNA vs GRID SEARCH: Bayesian optimization with 50 trials per 
   combination provides efficient hyperparameter search, exploring the 
   space more intelligently than exhaustive grid search.

4. RESAMPLING vs ENSEMBLE: While BalancedBagging consistently improves all 
   classifiers, individual resampling techniques (ROS, SMOTEENN, RUS) 
   combined with the right classifier can achieve comparable or better 
   results with simpler implementations.

5. INTERPRETABILITY CONFIRMED: Diagnosis_Delay is the #1 predictive 
   feature in both models, consistent with clinical literature and the 
   original paper. ALSFRS functional decline slopes are the next most 
   important features, highlighting the clinical value of longitudinal 
   functional assessments.

6. MLP FRAGILITY: Despite high CV AUC, MLP without ensemble wrapping is 
   fragile on severely imbalanced test data. This is a cautionary finding 
   for clinical ML applications.
