SECTION 1 — WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a REPLICATION + EXTENSION study of:
  Papaiz et al. (2024) — "An ensemble-imbalance machine learning model for
  the prediction of ALS prognosis." The paper used PRO-ACT clinical data
  to classify ALS patients as Short Survivors (death <= 24 months from
  symptom onset) vs Standard Survival, using 6 classifiers wrapped in
  BalancedBagging with 23 hand-crafted features.

Our study (by Ivo Camacho, supervised by Maryam Abbasi and Pedro Martins):
  1. REPLICATES the Papaiz et al. pipeline faithfully on a public PRO-ACT extract
  2. EXTENDS it with LightGBM (7th classifier), Bayesian HPO (Optuna), 10
     individual resampling strategies, a held-out test set, and dual SHAP analysis
  3. Produced a conference paper submitted to EPIA 2026 (Springer LLNCS format)

Folder: G:\TESE\CodigoMain\replication  (subfolder of main thesis project)

SECTION 2 — DATASET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source: PRO-ACT database (Pooled Resource Open-Access ALS Clinical Trials)
  - 16 raw CSV tables; publicly available extract

Target variable:
  Short Survivor  = died <= 24 months from symptom onset  (minority class)
  Standard Survival = died > 24 months, or alive > 24 months (majority class)

Exclusions applied (matching reference study):
  - Censored patients (alive < 24 months, outcome unknown)
  - Missing site-of-onset records
  - Missing FVC or height/BMI data (required for 23-feature set)
  NOTE: FVC missingness was 62.3% after survival+site filters — this is
        the main reason our cohort is smaller than the reference study.

Final dataset: 1,502 patients
  Short Survivors:    174  (11.6%)  — minority class, imbalance ratio 7.6
  Standard Survival: 1,328  (88.4%) — majority class

Reference study had: 1,967 patients (13.0% Short, ratio 6.9)
  Difference (-23.6%) due to more missing FVC in our PRO-ACT version.

Train/Test split: 80/20, stratified, random_state=42
  Train: 1,201 patients (139 Short, 1,062 Standard)
  Test:   301 patients  (35 Short,   266 Standard)  ← sealed until Step 6

23 features (identical to reference study, all ordinal/binary/categorical):
  Sex_Male, Site_Onset, Age_at_Onset (ordinal 0-4), Riluzole,
  Diagnosis_Delay (ordinal 0-2: short/avg/long),
  FVC_at_Diagnosis (binary: <80% vs >=80%),
  BMI_at_Diagnosis (ordinal 0-3), Patient_with_Gastrostomy,
  Qty_Regions_Involved (ordinal 1-4),
  Region_Involved_Bulbar/Upper_Limb/Lower_Limb/Respiratory (binary x4),
  Q1-Q10_slope (ALSFRS-R decline rate, ordinal 0-2: slow/avg/rapid, x10)

SECTION 3 — EXPERIMENTAL PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Data loading & exploration (16 CSVs from PRO-ACT)
Step 2: Feature engineering & preprocessing (produces 23 features)
Step 3: Cleaning & filtering (exclusions, final 1,502 patients)
Step 4: Stratified 80/20 train/test split
Step 5: Cross-validation model training
  - 7 classifiers: KNN, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, MLP, LightGBM*
  - 10 imbalance strategies: None, ROS, SMOTE, BorderlineSMOTE, ADASYN,
      RUS, NearMiss, TomekLinks, SMOTETomek, SMOTEENN
  - 70 classifier×strategy combinations
  - Optuna (TPE, 50 trials/combination), optimising ROC-AUC
  - RepeatedStratifiedKFold: 5 folds × 3 repeats = 15 evaluations per config
  - CRITICAL: resampling applied ONLY inside training fold, AFTER fold split,
              BEFORE classifier fitting. Validation fold and test set untouched.
  * LightGBM is our addition; reference study used 6 classifiers only
Step 6: Held-out test evaluation (retrain on full train, evaluate on test)
Step 7: SHAP interpretability
  - LightGBM: TreeSHAP (exact)
  - MLP: KernelSHAP (approximate, 100 background, 200 eval samples)
  - Computed on held-out test set after final retraining
Step 7b: BalancedBagging replication
  - Same 7 classifiers, 2 scenarios: Single-Model vs BalancedBagging (10 est.)
  - Same 5×3 CV protocol
Step 7c: Statistical validation
  - Wilcoxon signed-rank test (per-fold balanced accuracy, 15 folds)
  - Bonferroni correction: alpha_c = 0.05/7 ≈ 0.007

  DecisionTree  TomekLinks  0.874   0.628   0.286   0.970   0.526   10  25

  KEY FINDING — AUC-Sensitivity Decoupling:
    MLP has highest CV AUC (0.915) AND high test AUC (0.918) but only detects
    1/35 Short Survivors (Recall=0.029). The model correctly ranks probabilities
    (high AUC) but compresses minority-class scores below the 0.5 threshold.
    This is a consequence of optimising for AUC under severe imbalance without
    threshold calibration — not a model failure in terms of ranking ability.
