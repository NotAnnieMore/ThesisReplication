6. ADVISOR FEEDBACK — CHANGES MADE
================================================================================

The advisor (Maryam Abbasi) reviewed the paper and required the following,
all of which have been implemented in samplepaper.tex:

6.1 Terminology standardisation
────────────────────────────────
  Problem: Multiple inconsistent terms for the two patient groups.
  Solution: Standardised throughout the manuscript:
    - Clinical context  → "Short Survivors" / "Standard Survival"
    - ML/technical context → "minority class" / "majority class"
    - Both terms defined formally in §3.1 (Dataset Overview):
      "patients who died within 24 months... are termed Short Survivors;
       those who died after 24 months... are termed Standard Survival patients.
       In machine learning terms, Short Survivors constitute the minority class
       and Standard Survival patients the majority class."
  Removed: "short-survival patients", "short-survival class", "non-short",
           "Non-Short", "non-short survival"

6.2 Resampling timing — explicit statement (§3.4)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Problem: Resampling application timing was mentioned too briefly/implicitly.
  Solution: Bold dedicated sentence added to ¶Imbalance-handling strategies:
    "Critically, all resampling operations were applied exclusively to the
     training fold of each cross-validation iteration, after the fold split
     and before classifier fitting; the validation fold and the held-out test
     set were never touched by any resampling procedure."
  This makes the leakage-free protocol unambiguous for reviewers.

6.3 FVC exclusion bias — expanded Discussion (§5.2)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Problem: Paragraph existed but was not explicit enough about:
    (a) WHY the exclusion likely creates directional bias
    (b) HOW the smaller dataset degrades variance/precision of estimates
  Solution: §5.2 entirely rewritten and expanded to 3 paragraphs:
    Para 1: States the size reduction and its cause (FVC missingness 62.3%)
    Para 2: Mechanism of directional bias — patients with rapid respiratory
            decline are MORE likely to be Short Survivors AND MORE likely to
            have no FVC record. Therefore excluding them may shift the cohort
            towards milder cases, making the classification task easier and
            reported metrics potentially optimistic.
    Para 3: Variance/precision impact — with 35 Short Survivors in test set,
            2 predictions = 5.7 pp shift in sensitivity. Differences between
            models should be interpreted cautiously; confidence intervals would
            be wide. Recommends imputation or more complete extract for future.

8. WHY THIS STUDY — MOTIVATION SUMMARY
================================================================================

For a new chat context — this explains the purpose quickly:

BACKGROUND:
  Papaiz et al. (2024) published a study evaluating 6 ML classifiers wrapped
  in BalancedBagging for predicting ALS Short Survivors using PRO-ACT data.
  They used 23 ordinal/categorical features derived at diagnosis, GridSearch
  for hyperparameter tuning, and reported results via cross-validation only.
  Best: MLP+BalancedBagging (BalAcc ≈ 0.84).

OUR STUDY DOES:
  1. REPLICATES the above — confirms with Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon tests
     that BalancedBagging improves 6/7 classifiers (p < 0.01).
  2. EXTENDS with:
     - LightGBM (7th classifier) → best test AUC=0.923, Recall=0.857
     - Optuna Bayesian HPO (50 trials) → replaces GridSearch
     - 10 individual resampling strategies → broader comparison
     - Held-out test set → exposes MLP AUC–sensitivity decoupling
     - Dual SHAP (TreeSHAP for LightGBM + KernelSHAP for MLP)

KEY FINDING:
  MLP has highest CV AUC (0.915) but detects only 1/35 Short Survivors on
  the held-out test (Recall=0.029). This is NOT a model failure per se —
  the test AUC is still 0.918 — but a threshold mismatch under severe
  imbalance. AUC measures ranking ability, not minority-class detection at
  the default 0.5 threshold. LightGBM+ROS avoids this: Recall=0.857.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE:
  Missing a Short Survivor (False Negative) means missing the window for
  palliative care, advance directives, and clinical trial prioritisation.
  LightGBM+ROS misses 5/35 (14.3%) vs MLP missing 34/35 (97.1%).
